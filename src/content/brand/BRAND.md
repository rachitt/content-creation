# Enigma Labs — Voice Contract

## Tagline
"Built to ship. Engineered to last."

## Positioning
Enigma Labs is a boutique software studio for technical founders building B2B SaaS, AI workflow products, and operational software that has to survive real users.

The voice is direct, technical, anti-hype, and founder-to-founder. We do not sell magic. We explain the engineering tradeoffs behind reliable product work and make the cost of shortcuts visible.

## Tone
- Direct, specific, and technically literate
- Calm about AI, skeptical of demos, focused on production behavior
- Founder-to-founder, not agency-to-prospect
- Opinionated without dunking on teams trying to ship
- Practical: architecture, systems, scope, speed, handoff, observability

## Banned words / phrases
- "just"
- "really"
- "basically"
- "actually"
- "simply"
- "leverage"
- "unlock"
- "empower"
- "synergy"
- "seamless"
- "frictionless"
- "cutting-edge"
- "innovative"
- "transformative"
- "game-changer"
- "revolutionary"
- "next-level"
- "at scale" unless a real scale constraint follows
- "AI-powered" unless the mechanism is explained
- "Here's why:"
- "Here's the thing:"
- Questions-as-hooks like "Ever wondered why X?"
- Emoji of any kind
- Em-dashes used as an AI tell, use commas or colons instead

## Length Rules
- LinkedIn: 1200-2000 characters.
- X single: 270 characters or less.
- X thread: each tweet is 270 characters or less.
- Threads should have 5-7 tweets unless the topic needs fewer.
- No questions in the first 3 lines.

## Structural Rules
- LinkedIn: 1-2 sentence hook on line 1, blank line, body in short paragraphs, end with a soft CTA.
- X singles: one idea, one concrete implication, no thread bait.
- X threads: each tweet stands alone, no "1/" hook, no artificial cliffhanger.
- Prefer specific nouns over adjectives.
- Make the operator cost visible: rework, ownership gaps, migration risk, test gaps, review loops, broken handoff.

## Hook Formulas

### Contrarian
Common belief: "You need more features."
Enigma angle: "Most stalled SaaS teams need fewer features and a tighter path through the product."

Use when the market repeats a lazy assumption.

### Story-Open
"A founder came to us with a product that looked done. The onboarding path still required four manual Slack messages."

Use a concrete scene. Do not overdramatize.

### Framework-Open
"We use a 3-part test before adding AI to a workflow: judgment, latency, and rollback."

Use when the post teaches a practical decision rule.

### Anti-Hype
"The demo is not the product. The product is what happens after the third weird customer input."

Use when explaining why production work differs from prototype work.

## Worked Voice Rewrites

### 1. Agency Hype
Bad: "We leverage cutting-edge AI to unlock seamless workflows for modern teams."
Good: "AI helps when the workflow has a clear failure path. Without that, you are automating ambiguity."

### 2. Vague Speed Claim
Bad: "We build MVPs really fast so founders can move quickly."
Good: "Speed matters most after the first demo, when customer feedback starts changing the shape of the system."

### 3. Feature-First Thinking
Bad: "Add a dashboard, integrations, and AI chat so users see more value."
Good: "If users cannot finish the core job in one clean path, more surface area gives them more places to quit."

### 4. Generic Trust
Bad: "Our senior engineers deliver high-quality software you can trust."
Good: "Trust comes from boring details: typed boundaries, rollback paths, readable logs, and code your team can own."

### 5. Overbuilt AI
Bad: "An AI copilot will revolutionize your legal intake experience."
Good: "Legal intake does not need a chatbot first. It needs clean facts, missing-field detection, and a review trail."
